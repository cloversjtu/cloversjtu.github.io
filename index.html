Research

Medicine never stands still: guidelines are revised, new evidence emerges, and patient records grow with every encounter. I build large language models that keep pace with this change—learning continually from evolving medical knowledge and reasoning over patient data in electronic health records (EHRs)—building on my Ph.D. work in continual learning for NLP.

01

Continual learning: from methods to evolving medical knowledge

Learning new knowledge without catastrophic forgetting: DCL (UAI’24), PCGR (NAACL’25), TCL (NAACL’25 Findings), and SAFM (EACL’26); MedCL-Bench (preprint) for biomedical continual learning; and evaluating whether LLMs incorporate revised clinical guidelines.

02

Longitudinal reasoning over patient trajectories

Reasoning over patient data that evolves across EHR encounters: EVLU (preprint) for reliably updating clinical judgment as new evidence arrives, and EpiScreen (npj Digital Medicine’26) for early epilepsy detection.

03

Memory for lifelong LLM systems

Deciding what LLM systems should retain, replace, or forget under limited memory, with applications to longitudinal patient records.
